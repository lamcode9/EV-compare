00 · The adversarial pass

Red-team log — what v1 got wrong

v1 was reviewed by three independent critics: a fact-checker (every figure verified against 2025–26 primary sources), a creative director (design, story, emotion, mobile), and a hostile energy-systems skeptic (every link in the thesis chain attacked). Publishing the log is the ethos of the site applied to its own plan. The material findings:

FIXED

No human in the story. v1 had six acts of ascending scale and zero people. v2 over-corrected — it literalized the brand name into a “mom in Jakarta” protagonist and made SEA the spine. KM’s call, and the right one: humanity is the protagonist and the globe is the anchor; Southeast Asia is the sub-anchor, closer to home. v3’s human thread is the hearth — one human-scale vignette per act, each on a different continent, with a recurring “closer to home” SEA inset.

FIXED

No darkness before dawn. v1’s arc was awe → bigger awe → homework. v2 inserts “The False Dawn” — Vietnam’s real 2020–21 rooftop boom and crash — where the page dims and the HUD stops. Stakes before triumph.

FIXED

Art direction was genre-default (dark void + gold glow = every AI landing page since 2019). v2’s signature: the page literally dawns — begins in true black, ends in full daylight, and the daylight is the existing battery.mom UI. Also solves v1’s unaddressed jarring handoff from black/gold film to white/emerald planner.

FIXED

Mobile was a degradation target, not the medium. SEA traffic is 70–90% mobile on mid/low-end Android. v2: portrait-first storyboards, native scroll only (no scroll-jacking), no letterbox bars on mobile, ≤4 MB transfer budget, acceptance device = a ~$150 Android.

FIXED

“~8× more productive per panel in orbit” was indefensible as stated — it ignored beaming losses and compared peak irradiance. NASA’s 2024 OTPS study found beam-to-ground space solar 12–80× more expensive than terrestrial alternatives. v2 states 5–8× collected (per Google’s Suncatcher math), itemizes the losses, quotes the strongest critics on-page, and pivots Act IV to using power up there (orbital compute — which is now real) rather than beaming it down.

FIXED

“Effectively free” banned. Jevons paradox means abundant ≠ free — demand rises to meet cheap supply (that’s the point). And retail bills are mostly grid + fixed costs; the planner CTA promises control and payback, never free energy.

FIXED

Stale or wrong figures corrected — Shuttle ~$55k/kg (not $65k); US queue ~2.3 TW and falling (not 2.6+); BNEF Dec-2025 pack $108/kWh, cell $74; 2025 solar additions 647 GW; CO₂ 432 ppm; battery production 1.59 TWh on >4 TWh nameplate; and an honest new annotation — Chinese module prices rose ~30% in early 2026 on anti-overcapacity consolidation, proof the 2023–25 lows were partly dumping, not pure learning.

FIXED

Eight interactives was a solo-dev death march. v2 defines a four-piece “minimum awe-able product,” demotes the rest, and cuts sound design entirely (replaced with haptic ticks on Android — where the audience actually is).

FIXED

Acts IV–V read as e/acc boilerplate and “aligned intelligence” as a progress-meter gate was false precision. v2 merges them into one act grounded in deployed 2025–26 reality, demotes the Dyson swarm to a watermarked coda with its assumptions as the interface, and gives the AI-dependency gate a non-quantified treatment (“this gate has no dial we can read”).

KEPT

What survived attack: the Kardashev spine (K ≈ 0.73 verified), Wright’s Law as the physical thesis (solar 20.2%/doubling, li-ion 18–19% — robust), the six-gate bottleneck structure, the manufactured-vs-extracted asymmetry, and the “awe from truth” principle — which the skeptic concluded is best served by showing the counterarguments as charts rather than hiding them in footnotes.

01 · The ask, sharpened twice

The refined master prompt

Build “The Long Sunrise” — a mobile-first, scroll-driven cinematic experience for battery.mom that tells one continuous story: humanity’s 500,000-year relationship with energy, told at planetary scale and grounded by a recurring human-scale hearth — a different home, a different continent, each act — with Southeast Asia as the closer-to-home lens; the solar-and-storage revolution measurably underway; a false dawn that nearly killed it; the compounding loop of energy → compute → AI → machines → manufacturing; the first real hardware now computing in orbit; a speculative glimpse of the stellar endgame; and the specific, unglamorous work this decade that decides whether any of it happens.

Experience bar: AAA cinematic craft with one unforgettable signature — the page performs its own title. It opens in true darkness and, act by act, dawns: by the final scene the visitor is standing in full daylight, and the daylight is the real battery.mom interface, powered on and ready to plan their rooftop.

Interaction bar: A battery-shaped HUD that charges as the story progresses — draining once, at the false dawn — and reaches 100% exactly as it powers on the final CTA. A powers-of-ten zoom from a palm-sized cell rendered at true 1:1 scale to a planetary grid. A learning-curve time machine where the visitor makes the deployment bet themselves. A personal watt-card generated to share. Somatic beats in every act: hold the ember alive with your thumb; watch the screen dim to firelight; feel data land as haptic ticks.

Trust bar: Every number sourced and dated; projections visually distinct from records; the thesis’s strongest counterarguments shown as charts, on the page; a public ledger of dated, falsifiable predictions — including predictions against our own coolest ideas — where wrong calls stay visible forever.

02 · Why this story

Core thesis & emotional arc

The thesis, hardened: Every leap in what humans can do has been a leap in the energy available to each human — and for the first time, the next energy source is manufactured, not extracted. Coal, oil, and gas cost roughly the same per unit forever. Solar modules and battery cells get ~19–20% cheaper every time cumulative production doubles — a curve that has held for 45+ years through every prediction of its death. Modules have fallen ~99.9% since 1977; cells ~98.6% since 1991.

The honest refinement the skeptic forced: the learning curve governs the manufactured part. Modules are now a minority of system cost; permitting, labor, financing, and grids — the human part — don’t follow Wright’s Law. Australia installs residential solar at roughly a third of the US price for the same hardware. So the corrected thesis, which is stronger, not weaker: the machine part of energy gets cheaper forever; the human part is a choice. That choice is Act V — and it is battery.mom’s entire reason to exist.

And the pivot that turns the best attack into the brand: cheap solar cannibalizes its own midday value (capture rates falling, negative-price hours multiplying, curtailment rising). Cheap solar creates the problem; cheap storage is the business model that solves it. A site named battery.mom gets to make value deflation its hero chart.

The anchors (all verified)

K ≈ 0.73. Humanity runs on ~19–20.6 TW of primary energy — Kardashev 0.73. K1 (~1016 W) is ~500× today; K2, a swarm capturing the sun’s 3.8×1026 W, is ~20 trillion×. One number ties the ember to the swarm.

One hour of sunlight = one year of civilization. Earth intercepts ~173,000 TW; one hour is ~623 EJ — almost exactly annual world primary energy. The math is not approximate; it lands within a few percent.

One second of the sun = ~10,000× everything, ever. The sun emits more energy per second than humanity has consumed in its entire history — by roughly four orders of magnitude. This is why the story ends where it ends.

The emotional shape (rewritten): scarcity → ascent with a cost → a false dawn and real doubt → the measured revolution → the compounding century → a dream at the edge of dawn → waking into morning, with work to do. The visitor should leave with awe and agency — and the last thing they touch is the real planner.

03 · Narrative architecture

Five acts, an interlude, a coda

One continuous scroll, 6–7 minutes at reading pace, scene budget of one hero moment plus two data moments per act (hard cap), ≤8 viewport-heights per act on mobile. Every act is deep-linkable with its own OG poster frame and a one-line “story so far” strip for visitors arriving mid-story. The through-line characters: the sun (an ember → smoke-veiled → blazing → orbited → swarmed → morning light), the battery HUD (charging as civilization does), and the hearth — one human-scale vignette per act, each on a different continent, plus a “closer to home” inset that brings each act’s numbers back to Southeast Asia. The full ~1,500-word script is written and table-read on a phone before any engineering (Phase 0.5).

I

Fire — Half a Million Years of Tending

500,000 BCE → 1900 CE · K ≈ 0.4 · hearth: a fire pit, anywhere on Earth

True black. A single hearth gutters — the one image every ancestor of every visitor shares, on every continent. For 500,000 years, energy was a body (~100 W) plus a fire that died if you stopped feeding it. The price of light: one hour of reading light cost ~58 hours of labor at a Paleolithic fire; an hour of labor today buys ~350,000× more light than it did in Babylon. Energy was the ceiling on everything — lifespan, output, night itself.

Data moments: per-capita watts ladder (muscle → fire → animal → wind/water) · the Nordhaus price-of-light collapse in labor-hours · closer to home: the same collapse priced in hours of a Jakarta or Manila minimum wage.

Felt beat: hold your thumb on the ember to keep it alive — lift, and it dies. Then the screen itself dims to firelight brightness for one paragraph. Fire needed tending; that was the deal for half a million years.

II

Combustion — Borrowed Sunlight

1700 → 2010 · K 0.4 → 0.72 · hearth: the mill towns — Manchester 1850, Pittsburgh 1920, Shenzhen 1995

The hockey stick. Coal, oil, gas — ancient sunlight, fossilized, burned a million times faster than it formed. Energy per capita jumps ~10× in two centuries; the hearth becomes a furnace, and the same mill-town scene repeats across three continents and three centuries; smog is the price of the first ascent. Then the honest ledgers: CO₂ climbing the same staircase (now 432 ppm), and the frontier economies decoupling — US energy per capita peaked in 1979 and has drifted down ~15–20% since, while GDP doubled. Watts alone were never the whole story; energy services are. Efficiency is abundance too.

Data moments: the 10,000-year energy log chart erupting · Rosling-style energy-vs-prosperity scatter, animated over time — including the rich-country trajectories bending (the decoupling shown, not hidden) · CO₂ ghost trace · closer to home: SEA is climbing this exact staircase right now — coal is 47% of its generation, up from 37% in 2015.

Felt beat: “energy servants” — your body runs at ~100 W; a live counter assembles the ~25 invisible full-time human-equivalents currently burning on your behalf.

☾

Interlude: The False Dawn

1954 → 2021 · the only scene where the HUD stops · scene: three broken promises, then one close to home

The doubt beat, built from real failures. “Too cheap to meter,” 1954: nuclear then delivered a negative learning curve — costs rose ~3× with deployment (Grubler 2010). Launch costs stagnated for forty years. Concorde went to zero. And closest to home: Vietnam, 2020 — ~9 GW of rooftop solar in a single year, then the feed-in tariff ended and installations collapsed; panels curtailed, installers stranded. The page dims; the battery HUD drains; the K-odometer stops ticking for the only time. Exponentials are not destiny. None of what follows happens by default.

Data moments: the signature credibility chart — curves that held vs. curves that broke: PV, li-ion, LEDs, DNA sequencing against nuclear, Concorde, launch 1970–2010 — with the structural explanation (modular, factory-made, fast-iterating things learn; site-built, regulated megaprojects don’t) · Vietnam’s boom-bust bar chart.

Felt beat: the visitor’s scroll input momentarily yields nothing — the story refuses to advance for one beat — then catches, and dawn begins.

III

The Sun, Direct — The Measured Revolution

2010 → 2035 · K 0.72 → 0.75 · hearth: rooftops, everywhere at once

First light on the horizon — and from here the page only brightens. The verified record: modules $76/W (1977) → ~$0.09/W (2025); packs $7,500/kWh (1991) → $108/kWh, cells $74, cheapest LFP $50 (BNEF Dec 2025). Solar added 647 GW in 2025 — its +636 TWh was the largest annual generation increase by any power source in history; renewables passed coal in the world mix for the first time. The first terawatt of solar took ~68 years; the third took ~1.3. Then the site’s home truth: cheap midday solar cannibalizes its own price — capture rates fall, negative-price hours multiply — and storage is what restores the value. Grid batteries: +300 GWh installed in 2025, +51% y/y. The deployment ladder fills on every continent: cell → home wall pack → tower → factory → the 19 GWh farms. China installed 93 GW in a single month of 2025 — roughly a hundred panels a second — and Pakistan showed what $0.09/W does bottom-up: 27+ GW installed by households, half the country’s peak demand, no central plan. Closer to home, the stakes run highest: SEA demand grows +7%/yr while solar+wind sit at only ~4.5% — but climbing ~35%/yr.

Data moments: the Wright’s Law time machine — with the two axes honestly decoupled: cost-per-doubling is measured; how fast doublings arrive is your bet (logistic vs exponential deployment scenarios side by side), plus a shaded materials-cost floor the projection cannot cross and an annotation on 2023–25 overcapacity pricing (and the 2026 +30% consolidation rebound) · capture-rate decline → batteries restoring value (the hero chart) · installed-cost stack over time: module vs BOS vs soft costs, with the US-vs-Australia gap · solar seasonality vs latitude — Jakarta’s flat line vs Hamburg’s winter cliff: why the sunbelt’s 4 billion people electrify first, and why battery.mom starts in SEA.

Felt beat: the duck curve rendered as an actual sunset synced to the visitor’s real local time — the sun on the page is where the sun outside their window is.

IV

The Compounding Century — Energy, Intelligence, Orbit

2025 → 2050 · K 0.75 → 0.85 · hearth: a gigafactory floor, then a satellite bus

Brightening toward morning. The loop: cheap energy → cheap compute → capable AI → capable machines → faster manufacturing → cheaper energy. Drawn honestly: solid edges where measured (energy→compute: datacenters 485 TWh in 2025 → ~950 TWh by 2030; Big Tech capex >$400B in 2025 — now exceeding global oil-and-gas upstream investment), dashed where early (AI→robots→construction: Moravec’s paradox is undefeated; gigafactory lines are automated, building sites aren’t yet). Energy gates where and how fast compute grows; chips gate how much. And the loop has already left the ground — not as a render, as a manifest: Starcloud-1 put the first H100 in orbit (Nov 2025) and trained the first model in space a month later; Google’s Project Suncatcher launches prototype TPU satellites with Planet in early 2027; SpaceX has filed for an orbital-datacenter constellation. In orbit a panel collects 5–8× more energy than typical ground sites — collected, not delivered: the page shows the radiator problem (heat can only leave by radiation, ~300 W/m² realistic) and quotes the strongest critique on beam-to-ground space solar (NASA OTPS 2024: 12–80× terrestrial cost) directly. The near-term case is using power up there, not beaming it down. Jevons, owned in copy: we will use vastly more — that is the point. Abundance means energy stops being the limiting input, not that it stops costing money.

Data moments: the confidence-weighted flywheel (scroll-driven animation, visibly accelerating) · compute cost curves beside the energy curves — same Wright shape · launch $/kg: Shuttle ~$55k → Falcon 9 ~$2,700 → Starship targeting $100–200 (Suncatcher’s stated crossover: <$200/kg by mid-2030s) · “the case against” inset — the site arguing with itself, on purpose.

Felt beat: the orbital timeline is a receipts wall — dated mission patches, not concept art. The future begins as a list of things that already happened.

✦

Coda: The Swarm — A Dream at the Edge of Dawn

Undated, by design · 2 viewports · watermarked: INFORMED SPECULATION

The one dark-sky moment after dawn begins — explicitly a dream. If self-replicating manufacturing ever works (no element of it exists yet), exponentials do the rest: collectors thickening around the sun over centuries, K2, twenty trillion times today’s power. The interface is the assumptions — replication rate, ISRU efficiency — so the visitor sees the conclusion is assumption-driven, and no date is ever attached. Two viewports of wonder, one line of honesty, then the sky pales.

Felt beat: the sun — our companion since the ember — slowly gridded by drifting points of light, then washed out by something nearer: morning.

V

The Work — Morning, Everywhere

2026 → 2040 · full daylight · hearth: the visitor’s own rooftop, the meter running backwards

The page completes its dawn: backgrounds lift through first light into the white, emerald, familiar daylight of battery.mom itself. The gates that decide the century, each with current → required state — and SEA-specific gates alongside the global ones: TWh manufacturing (1.59 TWh made on >4 TWh nameplate — capacity isn’t the constraint, deployment is), grids and queues (~2.3 TW stalled in the US alone; ASEAN interconnects barely begun), materials and recycling, soft costs (the human part — Vietnam’s FiT cliff, Indonesian permitting, the US–Australia gap), cheap orbit, and dependable automation (no meter on this one: “unlike the others, this gate has no dial we can read”). Then the landing, back at the human scale where the story started — the hearth of Act I, now a rooftop: the battery HUD reaches 100% and visibly powers on three real cards — the home planner, the EV desk, the BESS desk. The swarm starts on a rooftop. Start with yours.

Data moments: the six-gate bottleneck board · the living epilogue: “since this page was published: +X GW, $/kWh now Y” — auto-fed monthly from the site’s existing data pipeline, with a “since your last visit” diff · the Predictions Ledger (§08).

Felt beat: daylight is not a metaphor here — the final viewport is the product, undecorated, ready to use.

04 · Scoped for one builder

Signature interactions — the MAP and the rest

v1 specced eight hero interactives; the critic correctly called it a death march. v2 defines the Minimum Awe-able Product — four pieces that carry the identity — and demotes everything else to scripted animation, pre-render, or post-launch. The most expensive v1 line (“scrub anywhere, the whole page re-renders”) is cut: the HUD is read-only.

The MAP — ships at launch

M1 · HERO

The Sun & the Dawn Arc

One WebGL shader sun, evolving per act (ember → veiled → blazing → orbited → swarmed → morning), keyed to the visitor’s actual local solar time — arrive at night and the piece opens at its darkest with “the sun reaches you in 3h 41m” ticking; the page itself brightens act by act until it becomes the real site. The swarm coda is this same object’s scripted final state — no separate simulator.

M2 · HUD

The Battery That Charges As You Scroll

Brand-native progress: a battery cell that accumulates charge across the story — slow through history, fast in Act III, draining at the False Dawn — reaching 100% exactly as it powers on the final CTA cards. K-number and TW readout ride inside it. Read-only; the scrollbar becomes the product.

M3 · ZOOM

Powers of Ten: The Battery Ladder

The continuous zoom — cell → a wall pack in a Southeast Asian home → tower BESS → factory → 19 GWh farm → grid at night → orbit. With the true-scale beat: mid-ladder, the zoom pauses to render a 4680 cell at physical 1:1 on the visitor’s screen. “This object, 4.6 cm wide, is what the next civilization is made of.”

M4 · SCRUB

Wright’s Law Time Machine

The learning curves with the two honest upgrades: measured cost-per-doubling vs chosen deployment scenario (the visitor makes the bet), a materials-floor band the projection can’t cross, and the overcapacity annotation. Bottom-anchored fat slider in the thumb zone — never a mid-canvas drag.

Supporting cast — cheap, scripted, or post-launch

S5 · SHARE

The Watt-Card

“Your Life in Watts” output becomes a generated, Wrapped-style card — your auto-detected country (with first-class SEA data), your draw vs. a medieval farmer vs. a K1 citizen, your rooftop’s share of the sunrise — rendered as an OG image with one share button. The distribution engine for the whole piece.

S6 · LOOP

The Flywheel

Scroll-driven animation (not a simulator) with confidence-weighted edges — solid for measured couplings, dashed for early ones. Honesty makes it more interesting, not less.

S7 · EARTH

The Terminator

Pre-rendered video/Lottie of the day-night line with one SEA close-up — not a live WebGL globe. Genre-default set piece; gets genre-default budget.

S8 · ALIVE

The Living Epilogue

Dated counters fed monthly by the site’s existing pipeline (“since this page was published…”), plus the Predictions Ledger cards. Turns a one-shot film into a serial that compounds trust.

Explicitly cut from v1

Scrub-anywhere full-page re-render (weeks of state machine for a feature few would grab) · standalone Dyson swarm simulator (folded into M1’s final state) · live WebGL globe (pre-rendered) · sound design entirely (replaced with navigator.vibrate micro-ticks on data landings for Android — tactile, free, default-permitted where the audience is) · the third typeface.

05 · Art direction

Visual & motion language — the dawn arc

The signature: a page that dawns

One bold move replaces v1’s genre-default “dark + gold glow”: the background performs the title. Act I is true black; the False Dawn is the darkest beat; from Act III the horizon lightens; Act IV brightens toward morning; the Coda dips back to star-field one last time (a dream); and Act V completes the sunrise into the existing battery.mom daylight UI — the paper/ink/brand/gold editorial system the site already ships (the June-2026 narrative-first revamp; reference page /state-of-battery-power). The film doesn’t link to the product; it becomes it. Gold remains the protagonist color, earned progressively; ember orange belongs to combustion; cool cyan is reserved for compute. Every glow is motivated by a light source in the scene.

Mobile is the primary medium

  • Storyboard portrait 9:16 first; desktop is the adaptation. Acceptance device: a ~$150 Android (Redmi/Realme class), tested on throttled 4G.
  • No letterbox bars on mobile — full-bleed, with title-card typography doing the cinematic work. Letterboxing is a desktop-only flourish.
  • Native scroll, always. No smooth-scroll libraries on touch; pins via position: sticky only; never intercept wheel or touch events. Scroll-jacking is the #1 reason people close these pieces.
  • Thumb-zone interaction: scrubs are bottom-anchored fat sliders or tap-to-step; never mid-canvas drags.
  • Hard budget: ≤4 MB total transfer on mobile including 3D assets; ≤200 KB critical JS before first scroll.

Type, texture, pacing

  • Two families only, both already in the codebase: Inter for everything, and the site’s existing display serif (Newsreader, already wired as font-display) for act titles. Zero new font payload; the film and the site share one typographic voice. Tabular numerals for HUD/data. (v1 specced “New York” — Apple-only; most of SEA would have seen Georgia.)
  • Numbers animate as odometer rolls, charts draw axes-then-trace-then-annotation; one hero animation per viewport, everything else still.
  • Pacing spec: full scroll ≈ 6–7 minutes at reading pace; one hero + two data moments per act, hard cap; a persistent act-skip rail; acts deep-linkable with OG poster frames and a one-line “story so far” strip when entered directly.
  • Texture: fine grain over the dark acts only (it fades with the dawn), chromatic aberration on the sun shader alone. Subtle or absent.

07 · Act V content

“The Work” — gates & honest constraints

Six gates, each a card: current state → required state → what unblocks it. Global numbers and a SEA row on every card — the audience’s grid, not just Virginia’s.

GATE 1

Make: TWh manufacturing

1.59 TWh produced on >4 TWh nameplate — capacity already outruns demand 2.5×. The gate isn’t building factories; it’s absorbing their output. Chemistry diversity (LFP, sodium-ion) hedges lithium.

GATE 2

Connect: grids & queues

~2.3 TW stalled in US queues (median >4 years); ASEAN cross-border interconnects barely begun; Vietnamese curtailment wasting built capacity. Wires and paperwork now bind more than physics.

GATE 3

Feed: materials & recycling

Silver thrifting, copper metallization, sodium-ion — the materials floor is real but bendable. By the 2040s retired packs become a primary mine; the loop must close.

GATE 4

Install: the human part ★

The star gate. Same hardware: ~$2.8/W installed in the US, ~$1/W in Australia. Permitting, financing, labor, and policy whiplash (Vietnam’s FiT cliff) are choices, not physics. This gate is battery.mom’s direct mission — and the story’s moral.

Lift: cheap orbit

Orbital compute is real but gated on $/kg (<$200 is the stated crossover), radiators, and rad-tolerant silicon. Beam-to-ground stays speculative until the OTPS math changes — we say so.

GATE 6

Trust: dependable automation

No progress meter on this card, by design: “unlike the others, this gate has no dial we can read.” The compounding loop assumes grids, factories, and machines that reliably do what we intend — stated as a dependency, linked to the serious literature, not scored.

08 · New in v2 — the credibility engine

The Predictions Ledger

Dated, falsifiable, public. Each prediction is a permalink card: claim, resolution criterion, source of truth, confidence, date published — and a status that flips to RESOLVED-RIGHT / RESOLVED-WRONG, with wrong calls left visible permanently. Two predictions deliberately bet against the site’s own coolest ideas; the willingness to be seen being wrong is the moat. Reviewed annually alongside the site’s monthly data updates.

11 · Guardrails

Guardrails, falsifiers & open decisions

  • Awe from truth — now with teeth: the strongest counterargument to each act appears on the page as a chart; two ledger predictions bet against our own thesis; banned-phrase list enforced in review.
  • One story, one scroll. A visitor who only scrolls gets the complete film in 6–7 minutes; interactives are depth, not tollgates.
  • Humanity is the protagonist; the hearth keeps it human. Scale without a person is a screensaver — every act touches down at human scale on a different continent, and Southeast Asia is the recurring closer-to-home lens, never the spine.
  • Restraint is the AAA tell. One hero animation per viewport; the polish pass cuts anything decorative.
  • Ends on agency, in daylight. The last thing the visitor touches is the real planner, not a metaphor.
What would falsify the thesis (published signposts)

We state, on the page, what would make us change our minds: the li-ion or PV learning rate breaking below ~10%/doubling for 3+ consecutive years on sustainable (non-dumping) prices · global storage additions stalling below 20% annual growth for 3 years · capture-rate collapse in high-solar markets without corresponding storage buildout · launch costs re-stagnating above $1,000/kg through 2035. If the signposts fire, the site says so — that’s what the Ledger is for.
